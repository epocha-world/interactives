Five human competencies that complement AI

The EPOCH framework, a labor economics model developed by Isabella Loaiza and Roberto Rigobon at MIT Sloan, maps out the exact human competencies that amplify and complement artificial intelligence. Rather than focusing on replacement, their research highlights the human-centric capabilities that fuel employment growth in the digital age. Watch a talk by Roberto Rigobon explaining EPOCH of AI.

Inspired by these findings, we took this learning and integrated it into the core of our leadership model, ensuring our trainees are uniquely equipped to thrive alongside artificial intelligence.

Spin, Reflect, Act

What is vision-boarding?

Vision-boarding is a visual way to show where you want to go. In this activity, you help build a collective vision board for leaders and teams who are driving innovation in IB schools across South Korea and internationally.

This activity is anonymous. No name, login, or email is needed and we do not ask for identifying information. Please avoid details that could identify you or others.

Why are we doing this?

Educators and school communities already do many great things. They also face real pressures, such as exams, academic stress, cultural differences, and fast change. Talking about these things helps, but talking alone does not change daily practice. We want to turn reflection into action. Together, your actions will become:

  • a set of practical ideas that you and others can try;
  • a best-practice reference built from real experience;
  • a reflection tool you can return to and ask, "What did I commit to? What happened?"

You will think about how you use the five EPOCH competencies (Empathy, Presence, Opinion, Creativity, and Hope) in your school, and how you approach the use of AI. Each question is linked to a real challenge in schools where that competency matters most, though many connect to more than one.

  • E: Empathy. Understanding what others feel and need, including yourself.
  • P: Presence and Connection. Being fully with others and building lasting relationships.
  • O: Opinion and Judgment. Forming your own views and acting on your values.
  • C: Creativity. Finding new ways to make learning work.
  • H: Hope and Vision. Believing in what is possible and helping others move towards it.
  • AI: Using AI. Using AI thoughtfully while protecting what is most human in education.
What you will do
  • Spin the wheel to get your question.
  • Think about your own work and your own school.
  • Write one action in response to the question.
  • Place your action on the vision board.
  • Share and listen to others if time allows.

You are welcome to spin more than once. For each question, write only one action. It can be one of two types:

  • CONTINUE: something you already do that works, and you want to keep building on.
  • START: something new that you want to try or learn more about.

Why only one? A single clear action is much more likely to happen than a long list. If you write five actions, you may not do any of them.

What makes a good action?

A strong action is:

  • Specific. Someone else can picture what you will do.
  • Doable. You can do it with the time and resources you have.
  • In your control. It depends on you, not on a decision someone else must make.
  • Visible. You will know whether you did it.
  • Time-bound. It has a rough timeframe, such as "this term" or "every week".

Examples

Not yet clear: "Use AI better."

Clear and concrete: "CONTINUE: Keep using AI to draft routine messages and spend the time I save running Story Circles with students."

Not yet clear: "Improve collaboration."

Clear and concrete: "START: Invite one colleague from another department for a coffee this month and share one challenge each."

These are examples only. Your action should come from your own experience.

A few tips

Will you do this alone, in pairs, or as a group?

  • Alone: spin, reflect on your own work, then write and place your action.
  • In pairs: spin together, take turns answering the question, then each write your own action.
  • As a small group of up to 6 (recommended): use a Story Circle format, described below.

Story Circle format

  • Spin. One person spins the wheel and reads the question aloud.
  • Round 1: tell. Each person takes a turn to share a short story or example from their own work in response to the question. Everyone else listens without interrupting, questioning or advising.
  • Round 2: reflect. After everyone has spoken, talk together. What did you hear? What connects your stories?
  • Act. Each person writes one CONTINUE or START action and places it on the vision board.

Keep the stories free of names and identifiable details, so that the circle stays respectful and anonymous.

Tips for writing your action

  • Be honest. There are no right or wrong answers. A small, real action is better than a big, unrealistic one.
  • Think small. Small actions that you really do are more powerful than large plans.
  • Choose an action you care about. You are more likely to follow through.
  • Both types count. Recognising what you already do well (CONTINUE) is just as valuable as trying something new (START).
  • Listen and read. Other people's actions may give you ideas for your own.
Keep it respectful and anonymous

Notice: Keep it respectful and anonymous. Do not name students, parents, or colleagues, and do not include private or identifiable information, about others or yourself.

Identifying details. Anonymous actions can still reveal who wrote them (for example, "at our school of 40 teachers in..."). We may lightly edit actions for clarity or to remove identifying details before sharing the summary.
